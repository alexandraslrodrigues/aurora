What’s in Aurora

Choose Cognitive disorders or Movement disorders on the home page. Each has three parts:

Aurora Report
A structured report for small vessel disease and brain atrophy. The movement disorders report is a work in progress.
Protocol
Recommended MRI protocols for a suspected cognitive disorder and for parkinsonism, from published guidelines.
Calculators
Visual atrophy scales (GCA, MTA, ERICA, Koedam) and midbrain and pons measurements for parkinsonism (MRPI, MRPI 2.0, ratios, MCP width).

Writing a report

  1. Start with Age and sex. The age is used for the age-adjusted MTA cut-off.
  2. Go through the sections with Next and Previous, or choose one from the list. Every section starts as normal: change only what you see.
  3. To leave out a section you did not assess, press × next to it in the list (or Exclude from report).
  4. Press ⓘ next to a title to see the definition, reference images and source.
  5. The last step is the Aurora Report: choose English or Portuguese, then copy the text or download an Excel file. Click a sentence to go back to its section.
  6. New report clears everything and starts again.

Questions

Is what I enter saved or sent anywhere?
Nothing you enter in a report or calculator is sent anywhere: it stays in this browser tab until you close it or press New report. Aurora counts page visits with Google Analytics, which does not see what you enter. Only messages you send with the message button reach us.
Can I use Aurora on my phone, or without internet?
Yes. Aurora works on phones, tablets and computers. After the first visit it also works offline, and you can install it from the browser menu (“Add to Home Screen” or “Install app”).
Which languages are available?
The website is in English. For now, reports can be written in English (US) or Portuguese (Portugal). More languages are planned, and help with translations is welcome.
Where do the scales and cutoffs come from?
From peer-reviewed publications. Press ⓘ next to a section, calculator or protocol to see its sources.
Can Aurora make a diagnosis?
No. Aurora is not a certified medical device. It helps you report what you see, and the interpretation stays with you.
