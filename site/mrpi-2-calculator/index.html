MRPI 2.0 calculator

A PSP marker that adds third ventricle enlargement to the MRPI. Designed to separate PSP-P from PD. The original study did not include MSA; in a later 3 T study, the difference between PSP-P and MSA-P was not significant after correction for multiple comparisons (Madetko 2022).

Open the calculator

Formula

MRPI 2.0 = MRPI × (third ventricle width / frontal horns width)

Measurements needed:

  • Midbrain surface area (mm²)
  • Pons surface area (mm²)
  • Width of superior cerebellar peduncles (mm), right and left
  • Width of middle cerebellar peduncles (mm), right and left
  • Width of the third ventricle V3 (mm), 3 measurements
  • Frontal horns width, maximal left-to-right (mm)

Published cutoffs

CutoffReported forSource
≥ 2.18PSP-P vs PDReference cutoff in Aurora; 3 T; sensitivity 100%, specificity 94.3%Quattrone 2018
≥ 2.50PSP-RS vs PD3 T; sensitivity and specificity 100%Quattrone 2018

Above 80 years, the reliability of published thresholds is uncertain: age-related midbrain atrophy can reduce specificity (Chougar 2024). MRPI is less affected by age than the midbrain/pons area ratio (Morelli 2014).

Published cutoffs depend on method, field strength and cohort. They support imaging assessment but are not diagnostic in isolation.

How to measure

Midbrain and pons areas

  1. True midsagittal T1-weighted image (through the aqueduct and the interpeduncular cistern).
  2. Upper blue line: through the superior pontine notch and the inferior edge of the quadrigeminal plate.
  3. Lower blue line: parallel to the upper line, through the inferior pontine notch.
  4. Midbrain area (upper red outline): the delta-shaped midbrain tegmentum above the upper line, closed by it; the tectum (quadrigeminal plate) is excluded.
  5. Pons area (lower red outline): between the two blue lines, bounded by the anterior and posterior margins of the pons.

Method: Quattrone 2008; Oba 2005.

Superior cerebellar peduncle (SCP) width

  1. Oblique coronal T1-weighted images, reformatted from a slab tangent to the floor of the fourth ventricle (red line).
  2. Starting view: the first image, moving anteroposteriorly, where the inferior colliculi and the SCPs are separated.
  3. Measure the distance between the medial and lateral borders of each SCP at the middle of its extension, on 3 consecutive sections.
  4. Enter the right and left widths (each the mean of its 3 sections); Aurora uses the mean of both.

Method: Quattrone 2008.

Middle cerebellar peduncle (MCP) width

  1. Parasagittal T1-weighted image that best shows the MCP between the pons and the cerebellum.
  2. Measure the distance between the superior and inferior borders of the MCP, delimited by the cerebrospinal fluid of the pontocerebellar cisterns.
  3. Measure right and left; Aurora uses the mean of both.

Method: Quattrone 2008; Nicoletti 2006.

Third ventricle width

  1. Axial T1-weighted image at the level of the anterior and posterior commissures.
  2. Measure the maximum distance between the lateral borders of the third ventricle at its anterior, middle and posterior parts.
  3. Enter the 3 measurements; Aurora uses the mean of those entered.

Method: Quattrone 2018.

Frontal horns width

  1. Axial T1-weighted image showing the maximal dilatation of the frontal horns.
  2. Measure the largest left-to-right width of the frontal horns.

Method: Quattrone 2018.

Abbreviations: PSP, progressive supranuclear palsy; PSP-RS, progressive supranuclear palsy with Richardson’s syndrome; PSP-P, progressive supranuclear palsy with predominant parkinsonism; PD, Parkinson’s disease; MSA, multiple system atrophy; MSA-P, multiple system atrophy–parkinsonian type; MCP, middle cerebellar peduncle; SCP, superior cerebellar peduncle; T, tesla.

About Aurora

Aurora gathers published scales and cutoffs to support structured reporting. It is not a certified medical device and does not give a diagnosis.

Aurora is described in: Rodrigues ASL, Lobo GG, Machado T, Pereira DJ. Aurora: Open-Access Web Application for Structured Neuroradiology Report and Score Calculation. AJNR Am J Neuroradiol 2026;47(2):419–423. doi:10.3174/ajnr.A8940.
